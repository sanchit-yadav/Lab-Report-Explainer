What the Test Measures:
ALT is an intracellular enzyme found almost exclusively in liver cells, making it a highly specific biomarker for hepatocellular injury.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
Elevated ALT signifies liver cell injury. Prominent causes include non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), viral hepatitis (A, B, C), medications (e.g. statins, acetaminophen), or autoimmune hepatitis.

What Low Values Mean:
Low ALT is considered normal and indicates no active liver injury.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- What is the primary suspect for my elevated ALT (fatty liver, medications, viral)?
- Would a liver ultrasound or viral hepatitis screen be indicated?
