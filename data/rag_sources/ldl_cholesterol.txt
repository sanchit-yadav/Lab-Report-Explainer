What the Test Measures:
Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL) is commonly termed 'bad cholesterol' because it deposits cholesterol into arterial walls, forming atherosclerotic plaques.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
Elevated LDL is a major causative factor in coronary heart disease, myocardial infarction, and stroke. Target thresholds depend on individual baseline cardiovascular risk profiles.

What Low Values Mean:
Low LDL is desirable for cardiovascular protection, though extremely low levels warrant clinical correlation for malabsorption or nutritional deficiencies.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- What is my personal target LDL goal based on my overall health history?
- Should we consider additional testing such as ApoB or Lp(a)?
