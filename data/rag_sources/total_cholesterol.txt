What the Test Measures:
Total cholesterol quantifies the cumulative amount of cholesterol in your bloodstream, comprising HDL, LDL, and roughly 20% of triglycerides.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
High total cholesterol (>200 mg/dL) is a recognized risk factor for atherosclerosis, coronary artery disease, and cerebrovascular events. Causes include high-saturated-fat diets, sedentary lifestyle, genetic familial hypercholesterolemia, and hypothyroidism.

What Low Values Mean:
Very low total cholesterol (<120 mg/dL) can occasionally be seen in malabsorption, severe liver disease, malnutrition, or hyperthyroidism.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- What is my overall 10-year cardiovascular risk score based on this lipid profile?
- Are dietary and lifestyle changes sufficient, or should we discuss medical risk management?
