What the Test Measures:
High-Density Lipoprotein (HDL) is often called 'good cholesterol' because it transports excess cholesterol from peripheral tissues and arterial walls back to the liver for excretion.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
High HDL levels (>50 mg/dL in females, >40 mg/dL in males) are generally protective against atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

What Low Values Mean:
Low HDL levels increase cardiovascular risk. Factors lowering HDL include cigarette smoking, metabolic syndrome, physical inactivity, obesity, and insulin resistance.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- How can I safely raise my protective HDL levels through exercise and nutrition?
- What is my Total Cholesterol to HDL ratio?
