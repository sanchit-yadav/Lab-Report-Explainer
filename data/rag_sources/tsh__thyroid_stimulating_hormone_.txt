What the Test Measures:
TSH is produced by the anterior pituitary gland to regulate the thyroid gland's production of thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3) through a sensitive negative feedback loop.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
An elevated TSH typically indicates primary hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid), where the pituitary releases more TSH to stimulate an underperforming thyroid. Symptoms may include fatigue, weight gain, constipation, and cold sensitivity.

What Low Values Mean:
A low TSH usually signifies hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid), where excessive circulating thyroid hormones suppress pituitary TSH secretion. Symptoms may include rapid heart rate, unintended weight loss, and tremors.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- Does my TSH result suggest an underactive or overactive thyroid state?
- Should we check Free T4 and anti-TPO thyroid antibodies?
- Do any supplements (such as biotin) interfere with my TSH assay?
