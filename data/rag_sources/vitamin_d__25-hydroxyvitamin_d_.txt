What the Test Measures:
25-hydroxyvitamin D is the chief circulating form of vitamin D, essential for calcium absorption, bone mineralization, and immune modulation.

What Low Values Mean:
Deficiency (<20 ng/mL) or insufficiency (20–30 ng/mL) can cause osteopenia, osteoporosis, rickets, muscle aches, and impaired immune function. Risk factors include minimal sunlight exposure, dark skin pigmentation, malabsorption, and obesity.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
High levels (>100 ng/mL) usually occur from excessive high-dose supplementation and can lead to hypercalcemia.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- What dosage and duration of vitamin D supplementation do you recommend for my level?
- Should I also check my calcium and parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels?
