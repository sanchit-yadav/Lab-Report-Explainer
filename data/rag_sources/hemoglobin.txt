What the Test Measures:
Hemoglobin is an iron-rich protein in your red blood cells that carries oxygen from your lungs to the rest of your body and returns carbon dioxide to your lungs to be exhaled.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
High hemoglobin levels (polycythemia) may be linked to dehydration, smoking, living at high altitudes, chronic lung diseases (such as COPD), congenital heart defects, or certain bone marrow disorders.

What Low Values Mean:
Low hemoglobin indicates anemia. Common causes include iron deficiency, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, chronic kidney disease, heavy menstrual bleeding, internal blood loss, or bone marrow suppression.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- What is the likely reason my hemoglobin is outside the standard reference range?
- Do I need additional iron studies, ferritin, or vitamin B12 testing?
- Should we retest in 4 to 8 weeks to check for stabilization?
