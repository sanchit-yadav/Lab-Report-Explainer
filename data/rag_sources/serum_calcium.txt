What the Test Measures:
Measures calcium in blood plasma, indispensable for bone structural integrity, cardiac contractility, nerve transmission, and coagulation cascades.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
High calcium (hypercalcemia) is most commonly caused by primary hyperparathyroidism or malignancy. Symptoms include nephrolithiasis (kidney stones), bone pain, abdominal pain, and confusion.

What Low Values Mean:
Low calcium (hypocalcemia) causes neuromuscular excitability, muscle spasms, tetany, and tingling around the mouth. Causes include hypoparathyroidism, severe vitamin D deficiency, renal failure, or low serum albumin.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- Was my calcium result corrected for my serum albumin level?
- Should we check my parathyroid hormone (PTH) and vitamin D levels?
