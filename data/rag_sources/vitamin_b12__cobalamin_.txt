What the Test Measures:
Vitamin B12 is an essential water-soluble vitamin necessary for red blood cell production, cellular DNA synthesis, and neurological nerve myelin sheath preservation.

What Low Values Mean:
Low B12 causes megaloblastic anemia and progressive peripheral neuropathy (tingling, numbness, memory impairment). Common in strict vegetarians/vegans, pernicious anemia, gastric bypass surgery, and long-term metformin or PPI use.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
High B12 can occur from supplementation or occasionally indicates liver disease or myeloproliferative disorders.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- Could my diet or medications (like metformin or antacids) be contributing to low B12?
- Would oral supplements or B12 injections be more effective for me?
