What the Test Measures:
ESR is a general, non-specific test indicating the presence of systemic inflammation in the body. It measures how fast red blood cells settle to the bottom of a test tube over one hour.

What Elevated / High Values Mean:
An elevated ESR suggests active systemic inflammation, infection, autoimmune disorders (e.g. polymyalgia rheumatica, lupus, rheumatoid arthritis), inflammatory bowel disease, or tissue injury.

What Low Values Mean:
A very low ESR is typically normal, but can sometimes be observed in polycythemia, extreme leukocytosis, or sickle cell disease.

Questions to Ask Your Doctor:
- What specific inflammatory or infectious process might be raising my sedimentation rate?
- Would a C-Reactive Protein (CRP) test provide complementary information?
